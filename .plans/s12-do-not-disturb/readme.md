# s12 · Do not disturb — a child's quiet is a preference, a parent's page is not

**Status:** planned (2026-09-21, late). The idea: a handset sets itself "do
not disturb" for 15, 30 or 45 minutes — no rings, no pages — except that a
page from the kitchen or the master bedroom still gets through. Drafted with
two Wi-Fi handhelds registered and the first PSTN calls a few hours old.

## What done looks like

A child in the theater dials `*7830`, hears "do not disturb for thirty
minutes," and for half an hour the theater phone does not ring: room-to-room
calls go to the theater's own mailbox, the house ring group skips it, the
theater's lobby extension sends friends to voicemail, and `500` pages pass
it by. A page from the kitchen or the master bedroom still comes through on
its speaker. At the thirty-first minute everything is normal again with
nobody having done anything, and if the box restarts in the meantime, the
worst case is a quiet phone that becomes a normal phone early.

The kitchen phone shows the theater's line key in red for those thirty
minutes, so "why isn't she picking up?" is answered before anyone dials.

## Starting point

- `500` is a dialplan `Page()` over every handset with `page = true`,
  generated by `render`; `0` (ring everyone but me) does not exist yet and is
  planned as the same kind of rendered group; room-to-room is a direct
  `Dial` from `extensions_handsets.conf`; the house ring group and each room's
  lobby extension are doorman's ring plans, originated over ARI.
- The WP826 has its own DND button. It is indefinite, it rejects everything
  including a parent's page, and Asterisk cannot see it. The s10 template
  can turn it off.
- Asterisk has a key–value store (`DB()`), presence hints, and the ability
  for ARI to evaluate dialplan functions on a live channel — which is how
  doorman can read a value the dialplan wrote without owning it.
- The plans README lists *composite prompts* (a clip, then `digits:`, then a
  clip) as a primitive with three customers waiting. "Do not disturb for
  thirty minutes" is the fourth.
- Per-handset voicemail is configuration today (every room shares `family`)
  and is where a DND'd room's calls should land; this stream assumes it.

## Decisions and invariants

**DND is a time-boxed key in Asterisk, set by the phone that wants it.**
`DB(DND/<handset-id>)` holds an expiry timestamp. `*78` followed by minutes
sets it for the handset dialling (the endpoint id comes from the channel, so
a phone can only silence itself); `*79` clears it; a value in the past is the
same as no value, so nothing ever has to clean up. Fifteen, thirty and
forty-five are the documented options and anything else is refused with a
prompt, because "DND for 480 minutes" is a phone that stopped ringing and
nobody remembers why.

**doorman reads DND and never writes it.** Before a ring plan originates a
leg, doorman asks Asterisk (through ARI, evaluating `DB()` on the caller's
channel) whether that handset is quiet, and skips it if so. The state is not
doorman's: a page is a dialplan feature that has to work when the daemon is
down, so the dialplan must be able to honour DND without doorman, and the
only way both can agree is if the dialplan owns the value. This is the same
line as the rate limiter and invariant 10 — doorman acquires no persistent
decision state — with the added point that this state is Asterisk's own,
time-boxed, and disposable.

**A page from an override handset always gets through.** `page_override =
true` on a handset in `handsets.toml` marks the phones whose pages are not
subject to DND — the kitchen, the master bedroom, whichever rooms the adults
are in. The rendered `500` group checks the *caller's* override before
checking each target's DND. This is not a convenience: it is what makes DND
safe to hand to a child. A phone in a bedroom that a parent cannot reach is
the WP826's own DND button, and that is exactly the feature being replaced.

**DND never touches outbound, 911, or the party line.** Dialling out is the
phone's own action; `911` is invariant 11's business and no state may gate
it; `600` is somewhere you go, not something that comes to you.

**Rendered, not hand-written.** Every group that must honour DND — `500`,
`0`, the room-to-room dial — is generated from `handsets.toml` already or
will be. The DND check is emitted by `render` into those groups; nobody
edits a dialplan to add a room, and a room added to the inventory is
covered on the next render. `doorman check` prints which handsets carry
`page_override` and refuses a config with none, because a house where no
phone can override is a house where DND is a wall.

**The phone's own DND is turned off by provisioning.** s10's template sets
the WP826's DND feature disabled, so the house has exactly one DND and it is
the one that respects the override. Until s10, `HUNT.md`-style
documentation says not to use the button.

**Presence is the display, hints are the mechanism.** Asterisk custom
presence per handset, driven by the same `*78`/`*79` codes, lets any phone
with a line key for a room show that room's DND. Optional, cheap once the
state exists, and the s10 template is where line keys get assigned.

## What is deliberately out of scope

- **Scheduled DND.** That is `[[schedules]]` and `afterhours`, which already
  exist and are policy, not a button. DND is the child's own thirty minutes;
  quiet hours are the house's rule. They compose: afterhours can be in force
  and a DND set on top of it changes nothing.
- **DND from the web or from Home Assistant.** The phone sets its own DND.
  If HA ever wants to, it goes through the same dialplan code, not a new
  interface.
- **Indefinite DND.** See above. A phone that never rings is a fault.
- **Per-caller exceptions** ("DND except Grandma"). That is the allow-list's
  job on the inbound side, and it already exists as the difference between a
  known caller and the lobby.

## Milestones and acceptance criteria

### M1 · The state and the codes

`*78NN` and `*79` in `extensions.conf`, writing and clearing
`DB(DND/<endpoint>)` with an expiry, refusing minutes outside {15, 30, 45};
confirmation and refusal prompts, the confirmation using the composite
`digits:` primitive (which lands with this milestone if s03/s01 have not
landed it first); `page_override` in `handsets.toml`, the schema, the LSP,
`llms.txt` and the man page, with the agreement tests; `doorman check`
printing override handsets and refusing zero.

Done when a handset can set and clear its own DND, cannot set another's,
the value expires without intervention, and the schema/docs agreement tests
pass.

### M2 · Honouring it

`render` emits the DND check into the `500` group (caller override first),
the `0` group, and the room-to-room dial (to the room's mailbox instead);
doorman's ring plans skip quiet handsets and send a quiet room's own
extension to its mailbox. Fake-ARI tests for the doorman side; rendered-
dialplan golden tests for the Asterisk side.

Done when, on jepsen: theater on DND, `102` from the kitchen goes to the
theater mailbox; `500` from the kitchen still pages the theater; `500` from
the theater (no override) pages the kitchen only; a known caller from
outside rings the kitchen only; the theater's lobby extension goes to
voicemail; and `600` and outbound are untouched.

### M3 · Showing it

Custom presence per handset driven by the DND codes; the s10 template
assigns line keys for other rooms; a DND'd room shows red. Done when the
kitchen phone shows the theater's state within a second of `*7830` and
clears at expiry.

## Alternatives rejected

- **The phone's own DND button.** Indefinite, invisible to the house, and it
  blocks a parent's page. It is the problem, not the solution.
- **doorman owning DND state.** Pages must work with the daemon down; the
  dialplan therefore has to hold the value; two owners would disagree.
- **DND without expiry.** A forgotten DND is a silent phone, which is the
  failure mode this project exists to prevent.
- **A "parent" role instead of a per-handset flag.** Roles are policy; this
  is inventory. Which phones override is a fact about where they sit.

## Rollout order

After per-handset voicemail (configuration; where quiet calls land) and the
`0` ring-all extension (the second group that has to honour DND). M1 and M2
can ship in one release; M3 waits for s10's line keys. Rehearsed by the
first customer's children, who will find the loophole nobody thought of.
