# s16 · The family voice pack — record the house's voice from a handset

**Status:** planned (2026-09-23). The children asked to record the lobby
greeting; the design that came out is smaller and more general than a
greeting: the bundled voice stays as the floor, a family's recordings sit
one layer on top, and any of them can be removed at any time to drop back
to the default. Recorded from a handset by dialling a code, with no software
beyond the dialplan.

## What done looks like

A child picks up the kitchen phone and dials `*99`. A calm voice says this
is the house's voice, six messages, hang up whenever. For each message she
hears the current one, a tone, records her own, hears it back, and presses
one to keep it, two to try again, or three to leave that one alone. Every
kept take is live for the next caller the moment she presses one. Later a
parent dials `*990` and the house is back to the bundled voice in a second —
or `*9902` to put back just the lobby greeting. `doorman check` says
`prompt pack: family (overlay on call-me-maybe; 2 of 6 recorded)`. Nothing
in doorman changed, no pack half-works, and the family's six files can be
copied out as a pack of their own.

## Starting point

- **The prompt-name contract is six clips** (`internal/lobby/prompts.go`):
  `welcome-known`, `lobby-greeting`, `invalid-extension`, `good-day`,
  `no-answer`, `connecting`. Adding a name is a breaking change for every
  pack, and CLAUDE.md says so. The bundled lines are in
  `prompts/manifest.json`; `prompts/build.sh` renders them with piper into
  8 kHz `.wav` and 16 kHz `.wav16` pairs.
- **doorman names prompts, it never opens them.** `Prompts.URI(name)` is
  `sound:<PROMPT_MEDIA_PREFIX>/<name>`; Asterisk resolves the file under its
  `astdatadir/sounds` and picks the best format for the channel. A missing
  file degrades to silence (invariant 7's "never a stuck call").
- **Asterisk can record from a handset** (`Record()`), play back
  (`Playback()`), and take a digit (`Background`/`Read`) — everything a
  recording booth needs, in the dialplan, with no daemon involved.
- s11 mentioned `*99` as the greeting-record code; s14's registry treats
  codes as bindings; s14 also established the pattern this stream reuses:
  **a second, optional clip set** that lives in the bundled pack and is
  never required of another pack.

## Decisions and invariants

**An overlay, one layer deep.** The bundled pack stays untouched at
`call-me-maybe/`. A `family/` directory beside it is *seeded* with one
symlink per clip (and per `.wav16` twin) to the bundled file, and
`PROMPT_MEDIA_PREFIX=family`. On day one the house sounds exactly as it
did. A recording replaces a symlink with a real file; restoring puts the
symlink back. A clip is therefore either a symlink (default) or a file
(family) and `ls -l` is the audit — the property is a filesystem fact, not
state. History is exactly one `.prev` per clip, for undo; nothing deeper.

**The `.wav16` twin goes when the `.wav` is recorded.** A handset records
at 8 kHz. If the 16 kHz symlink stayed, a wideband caller would hear the
default voice while a narrowband caller heard the family. Asterisk
transcodes up from 8 kHz; removing the twin is correct, and restoring the
default restores both links. The house will sound like a phone. That is the
aesthetic, not a limitation.

**Re-seeding follows the base pack.** Installing a different base pack —
purchased, or a rebuilt bundled one — re-points every *symlink* and leaves
every real file alone. The family's recordings survive every pack change;
that is what being a layer means.

**The studio is a second, optional clip set, never overlayable.** The
booth needs its own voice — eight short lines — and they are new prompt
names. They live in the bundled pack under `studio-*`, are generated by
`build.sh` from a `studio` section of the manifest, and the contract test
knows the section is optional: a third-party pack never has to ship them,
the bundled ones always play. And they are never themselves recordable: a
child who re-records "press one to keep it" would make the booth unusable
for the next child. The studio speaks in the house's default voice, always.

**The booth never names a clip; it plays it.** "Here's the message as it
is now" followed by the actual current clip beats any description, and it
removes six name clips from the set. What you are replacing is what you
just heard.

**Four codes, all dialplan, no doorman:**

| Code | Does |
|---|---|
| `*99` | the whole voice: all six, in the order a caller meets them — `welcome-known`, `lobby-greeting`, `invalid-extension`, `connecting`, `no-answer`, `good-day`; each kept take is saved immediately, so hanging up keeps what was kept |
| `*99N` | clip N alone, same loop once |
| `*990N` | put clip N back to the default |
| `*990` | put everything back |

Inside a loop: current → tone → record (`#` ends) → playback → `1` keep,
`2` again, `3` skip (leave as is), hang up = nothing changed for this clip.

**Who may record: any handset.** The hunt established who the content team
is. The floor cannot be damaged and `*990` is one call away; if it ever
turns out wrong, s12's `page_override` is the obvious gate and a one-line
change. A recording is logged as an event (`prompt.recorded { clip, by }`)
once s13 lands, so "who made the lobby say that" has an answer.

**`check` reports the layer.** One line — which pack is the base, how many
of six are recorded, and which — from a stat, not from state. `doorman
prompts` prints the same table with dates and lets the operator restore
from the workstation as well as from a handset.

**A family overlay is exportable.** The real files plus a manifest naming
which of the six they replace is a pack in every sense `PACKS.md` uses,
licensed however the family likes, with the bundled voice filling every
gap on any box it lands on. `PACKS.md` gains one section, "overlay packs,"
and the archetype rule is untouched — these are the family's own voices on
the family's own box, not distributed content.

## What is deliberately out of scope

- **A recording history.** One `.prev` is undo. A gallery of takes is a
  feature for a different product.
- **Recording the studio's own prompts.** See above.
- **Per-caller or per-time voices** ("the kids' voice after 3 pm"). Policy,
  not packs; and a family that wants it can record it into the message.
- **Doorman driving the booth over ARI.** The dialplan has every verb this
  needs and works with the daemon down.
- **Hi-fi.** 8 kHz from a handset, on purpose.

## Milestones and acceptance criteria

### M1 · The overlay

`doorman prompts seed` creates `family/` beside the base pack with the
twelve symlinks; `PROMPT_MEDIA_PREFIX=family` documented and set by `init`
once s09 owns it; `doorman prompts` lists clips as default/recorded with
dates; `doorman prompts restore <clip>|--all` from the workstation;
re-seeding on base-pack change; `check`'s one line. Done when the house
sounds identical before and after seeding, a hand-placed `.wav` over one
symlink is reported as recorded and played to a caller, restoring it is
reported as default, and a base-pack swap leaves the hand-placed file in
place.

### M2 · The studio

The eight `studio-*` lines in the manifest's optional section, rendered by
`build.sh`; the contract test amended to know the section is optional; the
`*99`, `*99N`, `*990N`, `*990` dialplan; the `.wav16` sibling rule; `.prev`
on every keep. Done when, on jepsen, a child records the lobby greeting
from the theater phone, a `*67` call hears it within a second of pressing
one, `*9902` puts the bundled line back, and a full `*99` pass with two
skips leaves exactly four files and two symlinks.

### M3 · Overlay packs on paper

`PACKS.md` → "overlay packs": the layout, the seed, the export; a note in
`LICENSES.md` that recorded overlays are the family's. Done when a
reader can export a family pack from `docs` alone and install it on a
second box, where the bundled voice fills whatever they did not record.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Overwrite the bundled files in place.** A pack reinstall erases the
  children. The layer exists so that can never happen.
- **A recording queue in doorman** (record via ARI, store, approve). State,
  a daemon dependency, and a booth that stops working when doorman is down.
- **Naming clips in the booth** ("the wrong-extension message"). Six more
  clips to maintain and less clear than playing the thing.
- **Adding the studio lines to the six-name contract.** Breaks every pack
  for a feature packs do not need.
- **Piper for the family voice.** The entire point is that it is not.

## Rollout order

M1 is small and self-contained. M2 is a day of dialplan and eight lines of
manifest, and it is the feature. M3 is a page. Rehearsed by the children,
who will find the loophole nobody thought of, as usual.
