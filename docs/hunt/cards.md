# The hunt — printable cards

Print this page, cut along the rules, fill in the blanks for your house, and
put each card in an envelope where its clue sends the player. The first card
is for the parent running it. Cards 2, 4 and 6 are *spoken*, not hidden: they
are what you record as the greeting of the mailbox named for the previous
answer (`docs/HUNT.md` explains). They are printed here so you have the words
in front of you when you press record.

These cards are CC BY-SA 4.0. They name nobody: the Keeper of the Kitchen and
the Voice in the Theater are whoever your family says they are.

---

## Card 0 — for the parent (keep this one)

**Before you hide anything:**

- Record greetings for mailbox `____` (chairs) and mailbox `____` (stairs).
- Tell the Voice in the Theater the password: `______________`
- Call Grandma — or whoever plays Grandma — and tell her the number of stairs.
- Put the handset on its charger in the kitchen. The hunt starts when you
  call the house.

**The one rule:** the answers are counts of things in the house. Never a PIN,
never a password the phone actually uses. A child only ever dials `*6` and a
number.

---

## Card 1 — on the kitchen table

*The phone rang and you answered it. Well done. That was the easy part.*

The Keeper of the Kitchen has a question for you:

**How many chairs are there at the table where this family eats?**

Count them. Then pick up the phone and dial **`*6`** followed by the number.
If you counted right, someone will speak to you.

---

## Card 2 — spoken (the greeting of the "chairs" mailbox)

> You counted the chairs. Now take this phone with you to the theater.
> Someone there is waiting. Dial **101** and ask them for the password.
> Say please.

*(Record this as the greeting of the mailbox named for your number of chairs.
Change `101` to the number of the handset in the room you mean.)*

---

## Card 3 — under a theater seat

*You have the password. Do not say it yet.*

Everyone who is playing — every handset in the house — should now dial
**`600`** at the same time. You will all end up in the same place.

When you can hear each other, **say the password together.**

Then look under the other seats.

---

## Card 4 — spoken (the greeting of the "password" mailbox), or hidden

> Somebody outside this house knows how many stairs there are. Call
> Grandma. She is expecting you. Ask her the number, and say thank you.

*(If your phonebook is set up, "Grandma" is a name on the screen. If not,
write her number on the card: `____-____-______`.)*

---

## Card 5 — at the top of the stairs

*Grandma told you a number. Was she right? Count them yourself.*

**How many stairs did you just climb?**

Dial **`*6`** and the number.

---

## Card 6 — spoken (the greeting of the "stairs" mailbox)

> That is the right number. There is nothing left to find. You have won.
> Dial **500** and tell the whole house.

---

## Card 7 — at dinner

*(Fold this one and put it under the winner's plate.)*

**You won.**

The prize is extra rice.
