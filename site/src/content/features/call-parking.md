## The problem

Someone calls for a person who is not in the room. The options are to shout, to
carry the phone, or to take a message — and taking a message is how a call for
one person becomes an errand for another.

Mobiles do not solve this at all. A call on your phone is on *your* phone; the
only handoff is putting it on speaker and walking.

## What parking does

Answer in the kitchen. Dial **700**. The call is parked and you are told the
slot number. Walk away.

The person it was for dials that slot from any handset in the house and picks the
call up where they are. If nobody collects it, it rings back rather than being
quietly lost.

## Why this feels like a real phone system

Because it is one. Parking is a standard PBX feature that every office had, and
it maps onto a house perfectly — the same problem exists, just with fewer desks.

It is the feature that makes people realise the house has a *switchboard* rather
than a set of extensions.

## Details worth knowing

**Parked calls hear hold music**, so the caller knows the line is alive. Silence
is indistinguishable from a dropped call.

**Timeouts ring back.** A forgotten parked call returns to the phone that parked
it rather than sitting there forever.

**Slots are announced.** You do not have to remember a number; Asterisk tells you
which one it used.
